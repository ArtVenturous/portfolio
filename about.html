Autobiography

Have ye ever wonder and asked thyself, Who am I? Well, 'tis a question we all ask ourselves at some point of our lives. Shall I ever answer that question, I answer thee 'I know not'. For I am among those who are lost, I wander being led astray. For I am among those who are blind, I see not the path I am walking. For I am still asleep, I lay and still not woke at dawn. For so ye ever ask me, I have not lived whatever such we call as life.

Born the eldest of poor family, I was given responsibilities at such a young age. I was only 8 years old when I started storekeeping. For ye see, my family's living was loaned from a bombay. While my father was unemployed, my mother didn't have a stable job back then. With three children having different needs and wants, my parents were really having a hard time. To earn extra living, my mother started a sari-sari store. Through my elementary years, every after class and when there were no classes I worked at that store. I remember I was only 9 years old when my parents first left me to run the store alone. It was no kidding —quite a tough work for a 9-year-old. I don't complain though; I did get my pocket money and every other needs from the store's income. The only thing I had about it was it felt like prison. 'Twas during those years, I grew up too fast. 'Twas the years I've lost my childhood.

Not long before those fateful years, I remember no pain, no worries, and no responsibilities. I remember the sweet taste of freedom, love and joy. 'Twas the time I lived with my grandparents. According to my grandmother, I was under their care immediately after I was born; the reason of which I know not. But for the time being, my grandparents gave me so much love and care. My grandparents lived in a big house and all day long I would play and roam around the rooms. Sometimes, I would play in the backyard where my grandfather tend animals such as roosters, chickens, and pigs. 'Twas quite fun.

When I lived with my grandparents, I had almost everything a kid would ask for— all that love and care and attention and all the time in the world. But though I had no worries, I was lonely. Imagine be it ye, a young child alone in a big house with almost no company. 'Tis true and why so might I say so? I was the only kid in the house. From time to time, my grandparents had other things to attend to and I was left to myself. When I do get out of the house, seldom shall I find other children in the neighborhood. I had lots nightmares about it. As a matter of fact, I never had any good dreams during my early childhood. Yet, I only lived with my grandparents up to the age of five and a half.

Quite the story of my childhood, isn't it —a lot of not-so-good stuff. There is something else. Perhaps one of the biggest turning point of my life was when my father had stroke. I remember I was in the fifth grade when it happened. Back at that time, we really had financial difficulties. On top of that, my two brothers got hospitalized for dengue too just a few months after my father's stroke. My mother couldn't pay the hospital fees. I had to open and close our store even when I have to go to school. What makes things much worse was I wasn't feeling well at that time. I was ill too but I didn't tell my mother. I just went through the illness as if it was nothing. Quite the bad luck, I had vigorous school activities to attend to also. I had to go through all that pain and burden, but thank God I made it.

Another turning point of my life was highschool. Perhaps one of the biggest choice I made was to stydy at PSHS-BRC. It was very far from my place. Ye see, I lived in Virac, Catanduanes and the school was in Goa, Camarines Sur. I had to cross waters inorder to get to the school. It was a tough decision, but the reason I chose that school was it offers more than the local schools in my home province. It offered me the chance of a better future. I was given the chance see the world.

Now why would I tell ye I have not lived, 'tis for this reason: I don't remember ever living my life. For so I was there when it was happening, I have not experienced it. For I know it happened, but I don't remember it happening. Because never do I recall living the present, I dwell in the past and the future.
